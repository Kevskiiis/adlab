AD DS Namespace and OU Hierarchy

This page covers the domain namespace, the Organizational Unit structure, and how it supports account administration, group management, and policy scoping.

Summary

  • Single forest and single domain: ad.comics.internal.
  • OU structure separating users, security groups, servers, and workstations.
  • Ten fictional employee accounts across four departments.
  • Group Policy scoped by OU, including separate Retail Operations workstation controls.

Administrative Behavior and Scope

Purpose

I use the OU hierarchy to isolate object types and support predictable policy application.

Implementation

Users, security groups, servers, and workstations are placed in their corresponding OU branches. Ten fictional employee accounts are placed in the department OUs under Users > Doomstadt. Everyone is a default security group for employees, while Network Shares holds groups for access to specific DoomVault folders.

Scope

Retail Operations is one workstation OU, and its computer controls are scoped separately from other containers. A few computer-security GPOs are scoped by OU, and user policies are coming soon under least privilege.

Validation

Policy behavior and effective access validation are in progress.

Design Decisions

Separate OU branches by object type

I separated object types into OU branches to support predictable policy application.

Retail Operations workstations in their own branch

Retail Operations workstations are in their own branch so their controls can be scoped separately from management and infrastructure containers, alongside other workstation OUs.

Production Considerations

This lab is not a production design. Outside a lab, these are the changes I would make.

What I would change

  • Review: Keep OU structure aligned to administrative scope.
  • Review: Document delegated administration boundaries.
  • Review: Review OU-linked GPO scope before applying changes.

Not yet addressed in the lab

  • Password and lockout policy.
  • Auditing and logging.
